Building Up to $769 Million

Digital spending hides everywhere in NYC's books. Our floor estimate layers three sources of confirmed spending — and the true figure is certainly higher.

Source: Checkbook NYC, FY2025. Floor estimate = $566M pure digital vendors + $203M digital portion of mixed vendors via high-confidence expense categories.

The annual budget for NYC's central technology office, OTI, alone is ~$800 million. Budget codes and expense categories systematically undercount digital work — categories named "Construction-Buildings" actually fund millions in software development.

A Decade of Growth in Tech Vendor Spending

Total spending on classified digital services vendors has grown from $827M in FY2016 to $2.2B in FY2025 — a 166% increase. Pure digital service vendors have grown fastest.

Big-Name Projects, Big-Name Price Tags

Six high-profile technology projects account for hundreds of millions. NYC Cyber Command alone has received $564M since FY2016, peaking at $116M in FY2023.

Source: Checkbook NYC budget codes. DOB NOW combines codes 6111 + NOW2. 311 shows IT vendor spending only (~15% of total).
For context Independent reporting estimated MyCity's cost at $100+ million, suggesting our analysis overall understates actual spending. The original CityTime project became a $700+ million scandal in 2011 — predating our data entirely.

Don't Trust the Category Names

Expense category names routinely mislead. "Construction-Buildings" sounds like bricks and mortar, but Spruce Technology and Prutech bill to it for DOB NOW system implementation.

Source: Checkbook NYC, FY2025. Percent shows share of category spending going to classified digital services vendors.

What $769M Could Fund Instead

State and local digital service teams achieve outsized returns on modest budgets. The U.S. Digital Service reported $3.5B in savings in 2020 from a team of ~200 people.

Methodology

Transaction-level spending data from Checkbook NYC for FY2016–2025 (29 million rows). Vendors classified via LLM-assisted research as "Digital" (primarily software/consulting) or "Mixed" (digital services bundled with hardware/infrastructure). Budget codes and expense categories cross-referenced with vendor classifications. Full methodology and data at github.com/henrytownley/nyc-tech-spending.
