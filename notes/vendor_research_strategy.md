# Vendor Research Strategy - Digital Services Classification

## Inspiration & Framework

This analysis is inspired by **Jennifer Pahlka's** vision for building government digital capacity, following the model of:
- **18F** - GSA's digital services consultancy

**Core Question:** What work is NYC currently paying contractors to do that could instead be done by NYC's in-house digital service teams?

## Goal
Determine if vendors are doing **digital services work** that NYC could bring in-house (like 18F model).

**Status:** ✅ 52 digital services vendors identified across full 5-year dataset
- 242+ vendors classified total (52 "Yes - Primary" or "Yes - Mixed", 218+ "No")
- $6.45 billion in digital services spending identified (FY2020-2024)
- Currently focused on systematic DoITT vendor research (129/358 completed - 36% progress)

**Approach:** Systematic research of DoITT vendors to identify digital services spending.

## Two-Tier Classification System

### "Yes - Primary" = Pure Digital Services Vendors
Vendors whose **primary business** is digital services:
- Custom software/application development firms
- Software platform companies (GCOM, NICE, Intergraph)
- Digital transformation consultancies (PruTech, Compulink, RCI Technologies)
- Systems integrators (Mythics for Oracle, MTX for Salesforce)
- IT professional staffing for digital projects (Experis)

**Confidence:** ~80-100% of their NYC spending is for digital services work

### "Yes - Mixed" = Digital Services + Other
Vendors who provide digital services **among other products/services**:
- IT resellers with professional services (CDW, SHI, Presidio, World Wide Technology)
- Diversified technology companies (IBM, Motorola Solutions)
- Big consultancies with multiple service lines (Accenture, Deloitte, KPMG)
- Specialty vendors with digital components (BlocPower, Vexcel)

**Confidence:** ~30-70% of their NYC spending is for digital services work (varies by vendor)

### "No" = Not Digital Services:
- Pure hardware/equipment sales without implementation
- Basic IT support/break-fix services
- Telecom/connectivity only (carrier services)
- Physical infrastructure (construction, cabling, electrical)
- Security guard services, facilities management
- Office supplies, printing services
- Nonprofits, educational institutions (unless doing tech development)
- Internal NYC agencies (not external vendors)

## Simple Search Strategy (Updated 2024-11-28)

**New Protocol: Efficient Parallel Research**

For each batch of 10 vendors:

1. **Initial Search (1 per vendor, all in parallel):**
   - Search: **"[Vendor] government digital services IT"**
   - This combines government work, digital services, and IT capabilities in one query
   - Run all 10 searches in a single parallel batch

2. **Follow-up Searches (only if needed):**
   - If results are unclear or ambiguous for any vendor, do additional targeted searches:
     - **"[Vendor] NYC"** - for NYC-specific certifications or projects
     - **"[Vendor] government"** - for federal/state contract details
   - Only search what you need to clarify

**Why this works:**
- Modern search engines handle combined queries well
- One comprehensive search often provides all needed information
- Follow-up searches available when needed without wasting time on clear cases
- Much faster: 10 searches vs 30 for initial batch

## Decision Rules

- **Clear evidence** of application/portal/platform work = **Yes**
- **Only mentions** of hardware/equipment sales = **No**
- **Mixed or unclear** = **Keep as Maybe** (it's okay to leave things uncertain)
